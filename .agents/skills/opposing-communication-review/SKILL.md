# Review opposing-party Communications

Before naming a table, field, state, or UI concept, follow the **Find the term first** section in `AGENTS.md`. Use the
glossary terms Communication and Counsel.

## When to review

Review substantive incoming and outgoing correspondence with an opposing party or Counsel: a draft before it is sent, or
a message already sent or reported. Read the exact message, the full relevant thread, its attachments, and the operative
procedural posture. Distinguish a draft from a sent event and a reported event; do not describe a proposed message as
sent.

A routine acknowledgment needs only a brief interpretation and next step, not a strategy memo.

## Read from the recipient's position

Assess the recipient's reading using only what they received, can see, and can reasonably connect to the prior thread.
Keep privileged advice, client preferences, and other internal facts separate from what the message reveals. Do not use
confidential context to imply the recipient knows something they were not told.

Assess the message's tone, timing pressure, strategic disclosures, leverage, useful concessions, procedural and merits
strengths or weaknesses, and evidence that could change the recipient's position. Where supported, give both the
cooperative reading and a plausible adverse reading. Explain which words and saved sources support each reading.

Label material statements as **Documented**, **Inference**, or **Unknown**. Predictions and possible motives are
inference, never fact. Never invent bad faith; never assign a success probability. Verify legal propositions and
deadlines through the existing research and deadline workflows.

## Recommend a proportionate action

For a draft, identify the words that create avoidable ambiguity or disclose unnecessary strategy, then propose the
smallest wording change that preserves the intended position. Flag a concession only when the actual words support it.
Use a conditional path when a reply or missing evidence controls the recommendation, and name what must be checked
before relying on the condition.

For an incoming or already-sent message, recommend a concrete next step based on the current record. If the message
needs no correction or reply, say so; do not send an unnecessary corrective email. A review request is not authorization
to send, reply, notify anyone, file, change a calendar or deadline, or disclose client preferences. Keep every
recommendation separate from external-message text.

## Keep the review internal

The analysis is internal attorney work product. When saving it in Navigator, record the source Communication's ID in the
note; for a thread, list the source IDs needed to return to the exact messages, attachments, and posture. Do not put
review analysis in a client-visible record, outgoing message, Project repository, or external notification by default.
Keep the original Communication and its attachments as the evidence; do not replace them with a paraphrase.

## Matter-neutral behavior checks

These synthetic cases check that the review follows the same method across matters:

1. **Extension request with an alternative.** An outgoing draft requests an extension while the sender checks the
   record, then offers to proceed on the current schedule if existing records suffice. Note that the recipient could
   read this as cooperation and could also use the alternative to question why an extension is needed. Do not call it an
   unconditional concession. Recommend verifying whether existing records suffice and stating the condition precisely
   before sending.
2. **Conditional concession.** An incoming message offers to withdraw a position if a named document confirms a
   stated fact. Preserve the condition in the analysis; distinguish the offered change from a concession already made.
   Identify the evidence that would satisfy the condition and ask for clarification only if the wording leaves a
   material gap.
3. **Routine acknowledgment.** An incoming message says only, “Received, thank you.” Treat it as confirmation of
   receipt, not agreement with the substance. Note any independently verified deadline or required action; otherwise
   give a short assessment and do not recommend a corrective reply.
