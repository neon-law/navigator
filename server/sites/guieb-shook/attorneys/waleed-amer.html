Overview

Waleed Amer is a business and securities litigator. He represents companies and shareholders in commercial and securities disputes in federal and state court, and advises businesses on contracts, business practices and avoiding disputes before they start. He also holds a Legal Engineer Certificate from Harvey Academy.

Before founding Guieb & Shook, Waleed was a senior associate at a New York litigation firm, where he was the lead attorney for Nasdaq- and NYSE-listed companies in complex, multimillion-dollar commercial and securities litigation, including disputes with institutional funds over the terms of their financing. His cases involved claims under the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, securities fraud, RICO and business torts.

He has led hearings and argued motions to dismiss, for summary judgment and for injunctions in the Southern District of New York, the District of Delaware, the District of Colorado and New York State Supreme Court. He has also taken and defended depositions, worked with valuation experts and negotiated settlements, and he works alongside in-house counsel and executives to keep each case aligned with the client’s business goals.

Earlier, as an associate at an insurance defense firm, he defended tort cases for insurance carriers from the start of a case through discovery. As a law graduate, he did pro bono work with Brooklyn Legal Services, for tenants displaced by a building fire, and with the Courtroom Advocates Project of Sanctuary for Families, helping survivors of domestic violence obtain orders of protection. He was a fellow at the Center for Business and Financial Law at New York Law School.

Outside the office, Waleed is an avid runner and a member of the Staten Island Athletic Club.

Representative experience

Before founding Guieb & Shook, Waleed:

  • Obtained a ruling in the Southern District of New York permitting service of process on foreign defendants through a Form 8-K filed on EDGAR, social media posts and notices in U.S. and international newspapers, followed by a default judgment requiring the defendants to retract defamatory articles about a publicly traded client and permanently enjoining further publication.
  • Obtained partial dismissal of a securities fraud action against a public company and its executives in the District of Colorado, including dismissal of civil conspiracy and Colorado Consumer Protection Act claims.
  • Obtained a stay of New York Supreme Court proceedings pending a related action in the District of Delaware, for a publicly traded holding company in litigation with an investment fund.
  • Drafted the opening motion in a tort action that ended in summary judgment dismissing all claims against a religious institution and a high school, on the ground that they owed the plaintiff no duty.
